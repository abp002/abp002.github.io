## Summary

Vonnegut said stories have shapes: how the protagonist fares from the first
page to the last. Those curves used to be drawn by scoring the tone of the
words, but tone is not fortune. story-shapes asks a model directly, passage by
passage, and draws the curve of 29 classics with its uncertainty band.

## Stack

- **Python**: chunking, reading and analysis.
- **Kev-4B**: an open model that answers with calibrated probabilities instead of text.
- **MLX and PyTorch**: Kev on a Mac and on a Kaggle T4 GPU.
- **matplotlib**: the curves and the video, generated from the same readings.

## Architecture

1. **Chunk.** Each Gutenberg book is split into passages of about 200 words.
2. **Ask.** Kev sees the protagonist's name and the passage, never the title,
   and answers how the protagonist is faring (five levels), how much tension
   there is and whether they appear.
3. **Draw.** The probabilities are smoothed into a curve with a band that
   includes the model's doubt.
4. **Publish.** The website shows each book passage by passage, with chapters,
   tension and what Kev answered.

## Notes

- It was measured before building: on passages where tone and fortune
  disagree, Kev is right 90% of the time and a word list (VADER) 18%.
- The main test fails and is published as such: with the rule fixed in
  advance, 2 hits in 27 books. Kev almost always points towards the right
  shape, but one book doesn't give enough passages to claim it with confidence.
